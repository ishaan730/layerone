    How to use it

    1. Name the request

      What's being asked for, and by which team.

    2. Answer four questions

      Each answer adds points; any answer that rules it out ends the scoring.

    3. Share the ranking

      The backlog sorts itself. Export it for your planning or docs.

    The four questions

    QuestionPointsRules it out when…
    Where is it needed?Main products 20 · smaller products 15 · one team 5It's needed nowhere yet
    How many teams can reuse it?Many 20 · a few 15 · one 5Only one product will ever use it
    Do we already have it?No 15 · partly 10 · mostly 5It's already fully solved
    How hard is it to build?Under a week 15 · 1–3 weeks 10 · 3+ weeks 5It isn't worth the effort

    55 or more is high: the system team builds it first. 40 to 54 is medium: accept it after high-priority work, ideally as a contribution. Below 40, defer: the team builds it as a local component, which can be promoted later if a second team needs it.

    The model comes from the CARS24 governance work, where one front door and a shared scorecard let any team see why a request was ranked where it was.

    Questions

    How do you prioritise design system requests?

    Score each one with the same questions: how widely it's needed, how many teams can reuse it, whether it exists already and how hard it is. Rank by score, and publish the reasons.

    What happens to low-scoring requests?

    They're deferred, and the requesting team builds a local component. If a second team needs it later, it can join the system.

    Can I change the scoring?

    Not in the tool yet, but the export gives you every answer, so you can re-weight in a spreadsheet.

    What you get

    • A 70-point score for each request, as you answer
    • A verdict: build first, build after, defer, or won't fix
    • A ranked backlog with the reasons visible
    • Markdown for your docs, CSV for a spreadsheet, or JSON
