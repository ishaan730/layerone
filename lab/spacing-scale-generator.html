How to use it

  1. Pick a base unit

    4px gives fine control for product UI; 8px gives fewer, clearer choices.

  2. Choose how it grows

    The common method adds the in-between values teams actually use, like 12 and 20.

  3. Try it on the card

    Change padding and gap from the scale, then export spacing and radius together.

4px or 8px?

An 8px base gives fewer, more distinct choices and suits marketing pages and simple apps. A 4px base adds the half-steps (4, 12, 20) that dense product UI needs for icons, tight lists and form controls. Many systems use a 4px base with a scale that mostly lands on multiples of 8.

Naming spacing tokens

T-shirt names (space-sm, space-md) are easy to read but hard to extend between steps. Multiples (space-4 for 16px on a 4px base) are easy to extend and to reason about. Pixel names (space-16) are the most literal. Pick one and use it everywhere; the scale matters more than the naming.

Questions

Should I use a 4px or 8px spacing system?

4px for dense product UI that needs half-steps like 12px and 20px; 8px for simpler layouts. A 4px base with mostly 8px multiples gives both.

How many spacing tokens do I need?

Usually 10 to 14. Fewer than that and teams invent values; many more and the differences stop being visible.

Should spacing use px or rem?

The export uses rem, so spacing scales with the reader's font-size setting, with the pixel value noted beside each token.

What radius scale should I use?

Four or five steps, plus a full radius for pills and avatars, is enough for most products.
