    Why OKLCH instead of HSL

    In HSL, "50% lightness" doesn't look equally light across hues: yellow at 50% is far brighter than blue at 50%. Ramps built in HSL end up with uneven steps, and contrast changes unpredictably from one colour to the next. OKLCH is designed so that equal changes in lightness look like equal changes to people, so a 500 in blue and a 500 in green behave alike.

    How the ramps are built

    Every ramp shares one lightness curve, from your lightest to your darkest setting, so the same step has about the same lightness in every hue. Chroma (colourfulness) peaks in the middle of the ramp and tapers at both ends, then each colour is fitted into the sRGB or P3 gamut without changing its lightness. If you paste a brand colour, it's pinned to the step with the closest lightness, so your exact hex appears in the system.

    Using the ramps in a design system

    Ramps are primitive tokens: raw values with neutral names like color-brand-600. Product code shouldn't use them directly. Map them to semantic tokens that name a job (color-action-primary → brand-600), so dark mode and rebrands become a change of mapping, not a hunt through every screen.

    Questions

    What is an OKLCH colour palette?

    A set of colour ramps defined in OKLCH, a colour space where lightness matches how bright colours look to people. It gives evenly spaced steps and more predictable contrast than HSL.

    Will my exact brand colour be in the palette?

    Yes, if you keep “Keep my exact colour at the closest step” on. Your hex replaces the generated colour at the step with the closest lightness.

    Can I use the palette with Tailwind?

    Yes. Export as Tailwind to get a colours object for tailwind.config.js, or as CSS variables you can reference from a Tailwind v4 theme.

    What is Display P3?

    A wider colour gamut supported by most modern phones and laptops. Choosing P3 adds oklch() values for those screens, with the sRGB hex as a fallback.

    Is it free?

    Yes. It runs entirely in your browser, needs no account, and saves your work locally.
