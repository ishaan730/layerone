How the design system team stopped being the bottleneck

As CARS24 grew, every team needed something from the design system, and one small team couldn't keep up. Requests piled up, nobody could see what was happening, and teams built around us. We changed how work came in, got decided and got built.

The 30-second version

The problem

Every request from every team ran through one small team. No front door, no shared rules, no way to help. Most requests just waited.

What we did

One front door, a 70-point scorecard anyone can apply, three ownership tiers, a contribution playbook and a steady listening rhythm.

What changed

Every decision came with a reason. Teams planned around releases, and more people built with the system instead of around it.

02

We tried the usual frameworks first

Seven angles of research, from shadowing to surveys. Then we scored real requests with RICE and MoSCoW. With RICE, people argued about the numbers; with MoSCoW, everything was a Must.

RICE: tried

Weighs value against effort, but reach and confidence are guesses for a shared component. The score became the argument.

MoSCoW: tried

Quick and familiar, but every team's request was a Must, and the label never explained why.

Our scorecard: used

Four questions a product designer can answer without us: where, who reuses, do we have it, how hard.

Tested in the open

Requesters scored their own asks next to ours. Where it felt wrong, we adjusted it.

Read the detail

We interviewed designers, engineers and PMs; mapped where requests got stuck; looked at unused and stalled requests; benchmarked Base, Shopify Polaris and Agoda; surveyed how much teams trusted the process; ran experiments; and shadowed teams to watch delays happen live.

04

A score instead of an argument

Four plain questions, each worth points. Any ✕ stops a request. 55+ is accepted and built first, 40–54 accepted, under 40 deferred. Try it on a request of your own.

Where is it needed?

Work in main products helps the most people.

How many can reuse it?

A design system is for shared things.

Do we already have it?

Filling a real gap beats rebuilding what works.

How hard is it to build?

Quick wins go first.

06

A system you set and forget falls apart

Listening was built into the routine at every speed, and a four-step playbook let teams contribute: propose, review and score, build together, document and release.

07
