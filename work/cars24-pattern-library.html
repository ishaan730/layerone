Work/Adoption at CARS24

Pages built from tested blocks, not from scratch

CARS24 was becoming a super app and launching new services almost every month, and every homepage and landing page was designed and built from zero. We built a pattern library that lets teams assemble pages from tested blocks, so they ship faster and every page feels like part of one product.

Context

A car company turning into a super app

By the time of this project, CARS24 was more than a place to buy and sell used cars. In one app you could get a loan, check a car's history, pay a fine, recharge FASTag, book a service or a car wash. New services launched almost every month, in India, the UAE and Australia.

The back end was keeping up. The screens people saw were not. Several redesigns were running at once, each with its own team, and the app was starting to feel like many apps stitched together.

  • Buy a used car
  • Sell your car
  • Get a loan
  • Car check
  • New cars
  • Insurance
  • Pay challan
  • Recharge FASTag
  • Car servicing

  • Scrap and earn

Some of the services sharing one app. Each needed its own homepage, landing pages and offer pages.

The problem

Every page started from a blank canvas

Whenever a team needed a homepage or a landing page, they designed and built it from scratch. Nothing was shared, so the same cards, buttons and carousels were made again and again, it cost more, it took longer, and the app felt different on every screen.

No shared direction

Every team made its own design calls.

The same work, again

Cards, CTAs and carousels were rebuilt by each team.

Higher cost

Design and engineering hours went into repeats.

Slow launches

New features waited for pages to be made.

The goal

Build a page once, reuse it everywhere

The goal was a reusable, scalable pattern library that helps teams ship consistent pages faster, with less effort, while leaving room to experiment without breaking the brand.

How we'd know it workedTarget
New or redesigned pages built from patterns50%+

Research

What we had, and what people already knew

We audited the homepages, landing pages and exit flows in every market, and sat with product managers, engineers and visual designers to collect the work they kept repeating. Layouts were all over the place, one component existed in many versions, there were no rules for content, and nobody knew how a section should adapt across screens.

Our users also used Swiggy, Zepto, Blinkit and District every day. Building on patterns they already understood, like service grids, offer banners and scrolling rails, meant less to learn. Service grids became quick-action rows, offer banners became the banner family, and scrolling rails became sliders.

Rules

Every pattern comes with its own rules

We started with the blocks every page needs: banners, grids, rails, lists and story widgets. Each one got the same four kinds of rules, so a content editor can fill a pattern in the CMS and still get a page that looks right. We also worked with engineering on performance, because a homepage carries a lot of these at once.

RuleWhat it fixesExample values
Grid structureColumns, gaps and scrolling, so every page has the same rhythm.12px gap; 2, 3 or 4 columns; scroll only when the next card peeks in
Text limitsEvery text slot has a size and a line limit. The CMS counts characters as editors type.18px section title, 1 line; 15px card title, 2 lines; 13px body, 2 lines
Image shapesThree ratios cover almost everything, so the CMS can crop and compress ahead of time.16:9 banners; 1:1 tiles; 3:4 stories
BehaviourSwitches in the CMS, not new designs: the same carousel can autoplay on one page and sit still on another.Breakpoints 360, 412, 768+; image, Rive or video; autoplay; infinite scroll

The library

Six families of patterns, and a toolbox around them

We turned the blocks into a modular library on top of the base design system. It came with layout templates for homepages and landing pages, ready-made combinations, content rules set up in the CMS, tokens and specs for every pattern, and guidance on which pattern to use when.

FamilyWhat it isUsed for
BannersOne template in three types: full bleed, padded and carousel, in fixed sizes.Campaigns, offers, the top of every homepage
GridsTwo, three and four columns, in landscape, square or portrait tiles, with one header and one button.Help and support, cars by body type, explore by brand
SlidersRails that scroll sideways, one to four cards in view, always with the next card peeking in.Hot deals, trending new cars, loans that fit
ListsTwo sizes for content that needs more words than a tile: thumbnail, title, one line and a chevron.Showrooms, car checks, fraud warnings
Stories and videoStory bubbles and video cards, with autoplay set in the CMS.Customer stories, expert videos, how CARS24 works
Snackbars and drop-off cardsSmall nudges that bring people back to what they left.Resume your search, finish your loan application

Handoff

One name from Figma to code to CMS

The library only works if four groups can use it without waiting on each other. Every pattern has one name everywhere, so a designer, a developer and a content editor are always talking about the same thing.

Designer

Picks patterns and variants and sets the order of the page. Works with contextual_card_slider.

Developer

Builds each pattern once, mapped to base tokens, and reuses it everywhere: <ContextualCardSlider />.

CMS team

Fills in images, copy and links, and schedules what goes live. Editors change content, never layout.

Product manager

Asks for new patterns with the request template, not a one-off screen.

Rollout

One library, every market and service

The library became the base for every major redesign: the India homepage, the UAE app revamp, the Australia homepage with its five themes, and the new service apps for loans, servicing, car care and car wash. Different colours and content, the same building blocks underneath.

Both targets were beaten. New pages took about 43% less time to design and build, against a target of 30–40%, and every major redesign was on the library within three months, against a target of half. Teams could test ideas without new builds, and the savings in design and development came to an estimated $212k.

Learnings

What we'd do again

Small, token-driven blocks scale

They made fast experiments possible without new design debt.

Follow what users already know

Familiar patterns leave room to innovate where it matters.

Docs decide adoption

Clear guidelines are what kept people using the library after launch.

Bring engineering in early

It caught performance limits before they became rework.
