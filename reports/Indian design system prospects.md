# Clicking first finds Layer One's best clients

**The notes support a full list of 100 primary prospects.** These are Indian product companies founded in 2015 or later that are not listed or acquired, and that have a product anyone can open without a sales call. The fintech, SaaS and consumer-travel firms near the top of the list share three traits: they are large enough to pay, their products have several surfaces, and they publicly show design investment that is visible from outside. Scale drives the raw score. Apna, Headout, InsuranceDekho, Moglix and Cashfree all score 80 or more. A separate ranking by evidence gives a sharper first wave of outreach. It favours companies that already run or are hiring for a design system and whose product can be audited today: **Appsmith** runs two design systems side by side on npm, **Eka Care** describes a design system that spans its EMR, PHR and AI products, and **SigNoz**, **Chatwoot** and **Hoppscotch** all publish UI or design-token packages. **Headout**, **Atlys**, **Sarvam AI** and **Keka** are hiring product designers right now. Of the 100 primaries, 86 are Freemium, 11 are Free Trial and only 3 are Public Demo. Self-serve B2B SaaS turned out to be scarcer than expected. About a third of the earlier "free trial" claims led to demo-request forms when checked, so consumer apps and developer tools now carry the accessible end of the list. A secondary list of 105 companies covers the attractive accounts that Layer One must reach through outreach rather than an audit. They are sales-led, inaccessible, listed, acquired or founded before 2015, and include Moneyview, Bounce, HealthPlix, Zopper and Darwinbox. Every figure below comes from aggregator or press sources. A manual click-through and a check against RoC filings come before any pitch.

## Method: page-level checks, not account-level tests

The research ran in two passes. The first pass, the sector files, was hampered by network blocks. Company sites, Inc42 and Entrackr failed to resolve, and web search was rate-limited, so most of its access claims were provisional ([e.g. Entrackr via search](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/moneyview-profit-grows-to-rs-240-cr-in-fy25-revenue-surges-74-10621058)). The second pass, the verify files, fetched pricing, signup, home and careers pages directly with curl. It read live job-board APIs (Keka embedjobs, Lever, Ashby), searched the npm registry and read Inc42 company pages. This second pass overrides the first wherever they conflict. **"Access verified" has a narrow meaning here: the public entry page loads and shows signup, login, trial or demo UI.** No accounts were created, and no OTP, KYC, quote or booking flow was completed. As a result, app-first consumer lenders, card issuers and home-services apps were scored as "Freemium-limited", because what sits behind the phone-number gate is unknown. Several sites blocked scripted fetches, including kissht.com, stashfin.com, uni.cards, rupeek.com, headout.com, truemeds.in and chargezone.co. These are marked unverified. The block itself does not mean the product is missing.

**Design-system evidence is systematically under-detected.** GitHub's org and contents APIs were mostly blocked (403), and Storybook subdomains and Dribbble were not swept. Most careers pages are JS- or ATS-rendered and return no role text to curl. A search of the npm registry across roughly 55 consumer-company scopes returned nothing. The positive findings therefore come from the few channels that were open: npm packages, the GitHub repo-search API and raw.githubusercontent, the Ashby/Lever/Keka job APIs, and third-party job boards. Where nothing was found, the CSV says "None found publicly (GitHub/Storybook not fully checkable)". That wording never claims a company lacks a design system. Employee counts are mostly Inc42 snapshots from November 2026, probably derived from LinkedIn. Some are visibly wrong: STAGE lists 47 staff against ₹111 Cr revenue, and PlanetSpark's 5,882 includes tutors.

## A transparent 100-point rubric weighted toward testability

Each score comes from four sub-scores, stored in `prospects_primary_data.py` and summed by `build_prospects.py`, so every number can be reproduced. Each CSV row records its breakdown in Additional Notes, for example "Score: Fin 13/25, Org 4/20, Access 30/30, DS 25/25". Where firmographic data was missing, the sub-score was set conservatively and the row is flagged "score limited by missing data".

| Component | Max | How points were awarded |
|---|---|---|
| Financial strength & funding | 25 | Revenue ≥ ₹1,000 Cr, funding ≥ $200M or a unicorn valuation: 22–25. ₹300–1,000 Cr or $100–200M: 19–21. ₹100–300 Cr or $50–100M: 16–18. ₹30–100 Cr or $20–50M: 12–15. ₹10–30 Cr or $5–20M: 8–11. Smaller: 4–7. No data: 3–5. Heavy losses cost 1–2 points; profitability adds 1. |
| Employee count & org scale | 20 | ≥ 1,500 staff: 18–20. 1,000–1,500: 16–17. 500–1,000: 13–15. 250–500: 10–12. 100–250: 7–9. 50–100: 5–6. Under 50: 3–4. Unknown or unreliable: 3–6. |
| Product accessibility & testability | 30 | Freemium with the full product on the web: 26–30. Self-serve Free Trial: 22–26. Freemium-limited (app-only, OTP/KYC gate, or free tools only): 18–24. Public Demo: 15–21. Gated or D-lean (form-gated trial, employer-enrolled app): 8–12. Site blocked or unverified: minus 3–5. |
| Product complexity & DS opportunity | 25 | Public or stated design system plus design hiring: 21–25. Design hiring across multiple surfaces: 17–20. Multiple surfaces, roles or brands without hiring evidence: 12–16. A single simple surface: 8–11. |

**Tiers:** High is 70 or above (19 companies), Medium is 55–69 (60) and Low is below 55 (21). The rubric's main weakness is that financial strength and headcount together carry 45 points, so large, well-funded companies rise above small teams with excellent design-system evidence. Appsmith, for example, scores 72 even with 30/30 for access and 25/25 for design-system opportunity, because its India entity reports only 48 staff ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/appsmith/)). This weighting is deliberate: budget matters for a consultancy. It is also why the quick-win list below re-ranks companies on access plus design-system evidence alone.

Twenty-three companies with A, B or C access scored below the cut-off of 48 and do not appear in the CSV. They are kept as a watchlist: Suraasa, Freo, Middleware, Pabbly, Mailmodo, Jiraaf, Gallabox, PayGlocal, Kylas, Decentro, Insurance Samadhan, Exly, Allo Health, Univest, Fi Money, Uni Cards, Grip Invest, BimaKavach, OneAssure, Fello, Liquide, Indiagold and Beshak. Most score low because their funding, revenue or headcount is missing or small, not because their products are hard to reach. Two companies the brief flagged as possible "D-lean" primaries, Pazcare and Nova Benefits, fell below the cut. Their only public surfaces are free calculators and an employee app, so they moved to the secondary list. Plum (58), Loop Health (56) and Onsurity (50) stayed in the primary list with their access scored as gated.

## Consumer tech, wealth/payments and dev tools offer the most to audit

The 100 primaries break down as **25 fintech, 23 SaaS, 15 consumer tech, 11 healthtech, 8 edtech, 6 mobility, 5 insurtech, and 2 each in jobs, proptech and B2B marketplaces**, plus one logistics company. Consumer tech has the highest average score, 67.4, with 5 High-tier companies. Travel and stays products (Headout, Atlys, StayVista, Elivaas, Zingbus) combine public booking funnels, real revenue and multi-sided products. Zingbus has ₹161 Cr FY25 revenue and a public seat-map booking flow ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/info-edge-backed-zingbus-crosses-rs-150-cr-revenue-in-fy25-10972155)). Headout reports $130M in calendar-2024 revenue ([Headout newsroom](https://newsroom.headout.com/news/headout-revenue-surges-to-130m/)).

**Wealth, payments and fin-SaaS split cleanly in two.** Self-serve merchant dashboards are directly testable. Cashfree's docs state that its sandbox works before account activation ([Cashfree sandbox docs](https://www.cashfree.com/docs/payments/online/resources/sandbox-environment.md)), and Easebuzz's registration dashboard is live ([Easebuzz](https://dashboard.easebuzz.in/register)). Free-plan SMB tools (Vyapar, myBillBook, Refrens, OkCredit) can be used in full. App-first lenders, by contrast, bring large revenue, such as **KreditBee's ₹2,185 Cr FY25** ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/digital-lending-startups-delay-ipo-plans-amid-sluggish-growth-in-fy25)), but almost no explorable surface before KYC. **Dhan** stands out among the fintechs. It became a $1.2B unicorn after a $120M Series B ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/news/dhan-turns-unicorn-after-120-mn-fundraise-led-by-hornbill-capital-10532947)), is profitable, and runs a public web trading app alongside mobile apps, an API and TradingView.

**Developer tools are where design-system evidence is most visible.** Open-source companies publish their front-end code. Appsmith's monorepo lists `@appsmith/ads`, `ads-old`, `wds`, `wds-headless`, `wds-theming` and a Storybook workspace ([package.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/appsmithorg/appsmith/release/app/client/package.json); [npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/@appsmith/wds)). Chatwoot has separate public ui, ui-components, design and styleguide repos ([GitHub search](https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=org:chatwoot)). The pitch to these companies is consolidation and migration, not a design system built from scratch. Horizontal B2B SaaS was the weakest segment for access. Kissflow, Qandle, HROne and Shopflo all turned out to be sales-led, and **Keka's "Free Trial" opens a demo-request form** ([Keka signup](https://www.keka.com/signup)), so Keka sits in the primary list only on the strength of its design hiring.

Healthtech and insurtech hold the clearest multi-role products: patient plus doctor, or HR admin plus employee plus claims. Only a few show design investment. Eka Care's free tier for doctors survived an August 2025 change to its limits ([Eka Care post](https://curiocdn.eka.care/services/important-update-to-eka-cares-free-tier-same-mission-clearer-limits)). Loop Health's designer listings describe "Loop App, onboarding modules, B2B interfaces as well as tools for efficient ops" ([General Catalyst job board](https://jobs.generalcatalyst.com/companies/loop-health/jobs/86370717-product-designer)).

## Quick-win audits: free products with visible design investment

For the first round of outreach, the better filter is access plus design-system evidence (out of 55), not the full score. Each company below lets Layer One prepare a specific, evidence-based critique before the first email.

**Appsmith** (55/55) is the strongest case in the dataset. It offers a free-forever plan and a 15-day Business trial with no card ([pricing](https://www.appsmith.com/pricing)), and its old and new component libraries ship in the same repo. A consolidation audit can be built from public code alone. **Eka Care** (51) has a free patient app, a doctor app and Mac/Windows EMR downloads. A job post says its designers contribute to "a design system used across EMR, PHR and AI flows" ([Keka job post](https://ekacare.keka.com/careers/jobdetails/135452)), and it publishes UI packages such as `@eka-care/medical-records-ui` ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/-/v1/search?text=%40eka-care)). **SigNoz** (50) publishes `@signozhq/design-tokens` while its frontend still runs Ant Design 5.11, which suggests a migration in progress. **Chatwoot** (49) and **Hoppscotch** (48) are tiny teams with public UI repos and `@hoppscotch/ui`. They are case-study material more than large contracts. **Headout** (50) and **Atlys** (48) combine public booking or application funnels with live designer hiring. **Sarvam AI** (45) offers a playground that needs no setup. **Dhan** (42) has a free web trading app, and a design-system check of its madefortrade.in community and developer docs is still to do.

Three named targets need a different approach. **Keka** shows the strongest evidence of an in-house design system in the set: it is hiring a Lead Product Designer and a Design Manager now, and in 2024 it advertised "Lead Designer, Design Systems" ([Keka careers](https://hr.keka.com/careers/jobdetails/75543)). Its trial sits behind a form, though, so audit the self-paced demos at keka.com/product-demos. **Loop Health** opens only a public app download page, so its hiring evidence has to carry the pitch. **Moneyview** and **Bounce** are on the secondary list only because they were founded in 2014. Moneyview's free credit-score product is a verified Freemium entry point, and its careers page refers to a "Design pod", UI designers and UX writers ([careers](https://moneyview.in/careers)). Bounce posted Product Designer and Senior Product Designer roles in September and November 2026 ([Ashby](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/Bounce/a787155b-7784-41df-9e5f-a845615a6aa7)). Both are worth auditing anyway. **HealthPlix** (founded 2014) is the other pre-2015 exception worth an audit: its speciality sandbox EMR loads without a login ([sandbox](https://demo.healthplix.com/sandboxSpecialities.php)).

## Data conflicts that must be resolved before any pitch

The sources disagree often enough that the CSV marks conflicts inline rather than picking a winner. **Revenue figures differ because the bases differ.** Jar's FY25 revenue is ₹208 Cr on an operating basis in Entrackr but ₹2,448 Cr in Inc42, which counts gross gold sales ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/news/jar-clocks-rs-208-cr-operating-revenue-in-fy25-turns-profitable-in-h2-10478229)). Stable Money appears at ₹4.3 Cr operating revenue and at ₹104–112 Cr gross. Inc42 page headers sometimes contradict the same page's financials text: Plum shows ₹13.9 Cr in the header and ₹69.9 Cr in the text ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/plum-1/financials/)). Truemeds' Inc42 figure of ₹8.3 Cr is plainly a parsing error against roughly ₹510 Cr net revenue reported elsewhere. **Funding totals vary by tracker.** Appsmith is listed at $49.0M, $51.5M and $56.5M, Stashfin at $424M and $458M, and Sarvam AI at $234M, $275M and $350M. **Founding years and HQ cities** are often incorporation dates or US holding-company addresses. Headout is founded 2015 or 2014 and based in New York or Bengaluru, depending on the source. Eka Care is 2020 or about 2016. STAGE is 2014 or about 2019. SuperOps, Hevo, Zluri, Scrut, Appsmith and SigNoz are India product organisations registered in the US. mPokket's FY24 revenue of ₹1,355 Cr is unusually high against earlier years and should be checked before anyone quotes it. Several 2026 events, such as RentoMojo's September 2026 listing ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/ipo/rentomojo-ltd-ipo-68302)) and Shadowfax's January 2026 listing ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/news/shadowfax-lists-at-9-discount-on-market-debut-11035898)), moved companies out of the primary list. Kissht, InsuranceDekho, Zypp and Kuku FM are all described as IPO-bound and need the same check close to outreach.

## Next steps

Four steps should come next, in order. **First, click through the top 30 and the quick-win set by hand.** Create test accounts where terms allow, record whether trials need a card or a phone number, and capture screens across web, Android and iOS to find visual drift. This turns "page loads with signup UI" into real evidence of testability. **Second, sweep LinkedIn and Dribbble for designers at each target**, check GitHub orgs and Storybook subdomains, and read Medium and Substack design blogs. The automated pass could not reach these channels, so it almost certainly missed design teams at Cashfree, Dhan, Zingbus and others. **Third, confirm financials against RoC filings** through Entrackr's Fintrackr articles or Tofler/TheKredible, starting with the flagged conflicts (Jar, Stable Money, mPokket, Plum, Truemeds, Supertails, Leverage Edu). Also confirm that each US-registered company runs its product organisation from India. **Fourth, re-verify status close to outreach**: IPO filings, acquisitions (Graphy's ownership, Fisdom's status, Hasura's pivot to PromptQL) and cash stress (Fi Money, Uni, Vyapar's falling cash reserves).

## Conclusion

The research changes the prospecting model in one important way: **open-source and developer-facing Indian companies give Layer One a level of pre-sales evidence that consumer and enterprise firms cannot.** A design-systems studio can open a meeting with Appsmith or SigNoz by naming the exact packages it would consolidate. For a lender or an HR suite, the best available is an inference from job posts. That makes a two-track motion sensible. Lead with audit-ready teardowns of design systems that are public or stated (Appsmith, Eka Care, SigNoz, Headout, Atlys, Keka). Use those as case studies to open doors at the larger fintech, travel and proptech accounts, whose budgets put them at the top of the list but whose products show their design-system needs only after an account is opened.

The second implication is that the "free trial" label on Indian B2B SaaS pages is unreliable. Roughly a third of the claims checked led to demo forms. Any future prospect list should therefore treat access as something to verify by hand, never as metadata copied from a pricing page.
