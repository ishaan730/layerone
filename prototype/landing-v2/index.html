The problem

Design debt doesn't announce itself. It shows up like this.

Nobody decides to have an inconsistent product. It builds up one reasonable shortcut at a time, until every change is slower than it should be.

  • #1D4ED8 · #2563EB · #3B6EF5 …color.action.primary

    Five blues in the code

    Every screen picked its own, and each was close enough at the time.

    A rebrand or a dark mode becomes a rewrite, not a setting.

  • DatePicker · Calendar · DateInputV2<DatePicker />

    Three date pickers

    Three teams needed one, so three teams built one.

    Every bug is fixed three times. Every accessibility issue ships three times.

    1. Date range picker 65
    2. Data table filters 55
    3. OTP input 55
    4. Promo banner 45

    One team for every request

    The system team turned into a queue, and nobody can see how it's ordered.

    Product teams stop waiting and build their own. The drift starts again.

None of these is a component problem. They're agreement problems, and agreements are what a design system is.

That's why we don't start by drawing buttons. We start by finding out what your teams have already agreed on, and what they haven't.

What we build

A design system has four layers. Most teams only build one.

  1. One source for every colour, size and space, named for its job. A rebrand, a dark mode or a second market becomes a change of values instead of a rewrite.

    • OKLCH palette
    • primitive → semantic → component
    • themes

  2. The parts your teams reach for every day, with every state designed, identical in Figma and in code. One date picker, fixed once.

    • 12 core components
    • mapped 1:1 to code
    • accessible by default

  3. Agreed answers for forms, tables, empty states and errors, so each team stops re-debating them and every screen feels like the same product.

    • forms
    • lists and tables
    • feedback states

  4. Who decides, how requests are ranked, how changes ship without breaking anyone. The layer that stops the system team becoming the bottleneck.

    • one front door
    • request scorecard
    • contribution playbook

Questions

What teams ask before they start.

Ask something else
You're a new studio. Why should we trust you?

Because the work isn't new. The methods come from six years of building and running design systems at CARS24, IBM and JPMorgan Chase, and the case studies show the decisions and the numbers. Layer One opened in 2026, so we have no studio client logos yet, and we won't pretend otherwise. It's also why the audit is small, fixed and credited: you can test how we work for $1,500 before committing to anything larger.

We already have a design system. Is this still for us?

Usually, yes. Most teams who call us have a library but not a system: components exist, but tokens are inconsistent, Figma and code have drifted, or nobody is sure who decides. The audit tells you which of those is costing you most, and the Partner engagement is built for systems that need running rather than starting.

What exactly do we get from the two-week audit?

A count of every style, token and component you have in Figma and in code; a scan for hard-coded values and forked components; a contrast check of your key flows; a maturity score; and a ranked roadmap with effort and impact, walked through with your leads in a 60-minute readout. It's useful on its own, whoever does the work next.

Do you work with our engineers, or only in Figma?

Both. We work in your Figma files and your repository, alongside your engineers. Every component in Figma is mapped one-to-one to its coded counterpart, with the same names and properties.

Where are you based, and how do we stay in step?

Bangalore, India, with daily overlap for Gulf, European and US teams. Every engagement has a weekly demo, a shared channel and written decisions, so nothing depends on being in the same room or the same time zone.

What happens when the engagement ends?

You own every file, token and component from day one. Foundations ends with documentation, a handover workshop and 30 days of support, and our last rule is the one we're strictest about: leave the team able to run it without us.

What is a founding partner?

Our first three studio clients, starting in 2027. You get 20% off any engagement and a team that treats your system as its flagship. In return, we publish the case study together. See the details.
